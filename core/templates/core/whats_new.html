What's new in Y-Video

If you taught with the previous version of Y-Video, most of what you know still applies. A few things were renamed, and a few things you used to work around are now built in. Here is what changed.

Works on iPhone, iPad, and Safari

The previous version of Y-Video could not play videos in Safari or in any browser on iPhone or iPad. Students can now watch on those devices, including in fullscreen.

Collections are now ‘Playlists’

What you built as a collection is called a playlist now. Nothing about how you use it changed: it is still the shelf you put media on and hand to a class.

Edit overlays and subtitles are shareable

The edits you make to a video are stored in an edit overlay, and that overlay is no longer locked to one video. An overlay is linked to the underlying media, so the same overlay can be used by any video built from that media — yours or a colleague's.

When you open the editor you can:

  • Create a new, empty overlay.
  • Use an existing overlay that you or someone else already made for this media. All future changes to this overlay are automatically applied to your video.
  • Copy from an existing overlay when you want your own version to diverge from theirs.
  • Import an overlay from a file, and export one to hand off.

Subtitle tracks are linked to the media too: a track made for a film is available in every video built from that film, and you can copy one to make your own version.

Three editors combined into one

The three editors in the old Y-Video (video editor, clip editor, and subtitle editor) are now combined into one. Each edit type is color-coded and can be grouped in custom tracks in the timeline. Edits save as you make them and can be undone and redone, either with the toolbar buttons or with Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z (Cmd+Z and Cmd+Shift+Z on a Mac).

Safer options for Fair Use

After you define clips in the editor, turn on Clips only under Settings on the video's details page to keep students within those clips. This gives much stronger protection for Fair Use's definitions of "reasonable or limited amount" or "short portions" of a work.

Request media inside Y-Video

Instructors can request a new film with Request a resource in the menu. You still need to bring your physical copy (DVD, Blu-ray, or VHS) to the Humanities Learning Commons (HLC) as proof of ownership.

Custom thumbnails

Videos and playlists can have a custom thumbnail: a solid color, a frame from the video (or YouTube's own thumbnail for YouTube videos), or an image you upload, with optional text on top.

Link straight from your syllabus

Every video has its own address. Copy it from your browser while viewing the video and paste it into your syllabus or Learning Suite to send students directly to it. Students are asked to sign in first, then taken to the video.

Still stuck?

If something you relied on seems to be missing, please ask. We are actively developing the new system and can often add requested features quickly. Send us your questions and suggestions through .
